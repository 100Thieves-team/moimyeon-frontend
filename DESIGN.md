## Overview

Dia Browser uses quiet, high-contrast product styling: soft off-white surfaces,
near-black text, restrained translucency, compact floating controls, and
saturated accent colors for brand moments. The system should feel precise,
modern, and focused without decorative noise.

The visual character is close to white stationery with prismatic edges: most of
the interface stays monochrome, while color appears in concentrated moments.
Hierarchy should come from typography, surface contrast, and product imagery
rather than decorative panels.

This reference covers the light and dark token sets, a semantic type scale,
elevation, and motion. It is the visual expression of the strategy described in
Design Principles below: quiet, familiar core surfaces, with expressive styling
reserved for concentrated brand and AI moments.

## Design Principles

Dia's public design strategy frames the system in four principles. They explain
why the tokens in this reference look the way they do.

- **Familiar, but elevated.** Core UI relies on patterns people already know,
  then lifts them with craft — fluid motion, careful polish, details like the
  active tab's color extending into the chrome. The goal is that anyone can
  switch to Dia on an ordinary weekday morning without relearning their
  browser. In this system that means conventional monochrome surfaces and
  restrained, underline-emphasis links.
- **Novelty budget.** People have limited capacity to learn new interactions,
  so expressive design is spent deliberately — on Chat/AI and brand moments,
  not on core browsing. This is the rationale behind the rule that saturated
  color appears only in small, concentrated accent moments while the rest of
  the interface stays monochrome.
- **Calm minimalism.** One obvious path per task; no duplicate UI routes; no
  interruptive chrome such as update banners. Secondary controls appear on
  hover or intent rather than sitting permanently in view. Visually this maps
  to quiet shadows, light borders, and the absence of heavy card panels.
- **Humanized craft.** Motion and shape keep the product approachable rather
  than clinical: the site easing curve, soft controlled corner radii, and
  frosted, layered depth.

Source: The Browser Company, "The strategy behind Dia's design"
(https://browsercompany.substack.com/p/the-strategy-behind-dias-design) — the
phrase "familiar, but elevated" is theirs.

## Colors

The palette is built on an off-white page surface, alpha-black semantic roles,
and saturated red, blue, pink, yellow, and brown accents. The token named
`white` represents the off-white surface `#F8F8F8`, not pure white.
Pure white is the formal token `true-white` (`#FFFFFF`), reserved for
high-contrast UI moments such as the hero CTA and raised cards. Saturated
colors should read as accents instead of broad solid fills.

Surface roles:

- Page and floating navigation surface: `#F8F8F8`
- Hero background: `#020204`
- Hero download button surface: `#FFFFFF` (`true-white`)
- Final download button surface: `#000000`
- Privacy link surface: `#EFEFEF` (`light-grey`)
- Card surface: `#FFFFFF` (`true-white`)
- Navigation border: `rgba(0,0,0,0.08)`
- Body text: `rgba(0,0,0,0.85)`
- Secondary copy: `rgba(0,0,0,0.60)`
- Footer link text: `rgba(0,0,0,0.425)`

Dark mode swaps the alpha-black semantic roles for alpha-white equivalents over
a `#1B1B1B` background (see the `dark` token block). Apply the dark block
wholesale — do not mix individual dark values into the light theme. Accent
colors stay identical in both modes.

Two smaller notes: inline links may shift to the dedicated `link-hover` indigo
on hover, and the source site defines its header background twice
(`#dddddd77` in variables, `#EBEBEBB3` in the utility layer) — this reference
adopts the utility value.

### Rules

- The larger the heading, the lighter the weight and the tighter the tracking:
  `h1`–`h4` render at weight `300` with `-0.04em` to `-0.02em`. Never bold
  large headings.
- High weight is reserved for `display` moments only.
- Metadata and captions are uppercase mono with wide `+0.1em` tracking.

## Layout

The system uses a 1rem = 10px convention (the source site sets
`html { font-size: 62.5% }`), so every rem value in this reference reads as
pixels ÷ 10 — `1.6rem` is 16px.

Breakpoints are simplified to five semantic tiers. The source site uses numeric
100px-step prefixes (400–2000); this reference adopts only the dominant switch
points:

- `sm` 600px — enter tablet
- `md` 800px — primary switch point; `h1`–`h3` and `display` move toward
  desktop sizes
- `lg` 1000px — `h4` and `p1` move to desktop sizes
- `xl` 1200px — wide layout adjustments
- `2xl` 1400px — ultra-wide refinements

2000px is not a breakpoint; it is the container ceiling (`layout.maxWidth`).
When porting a style from the source site, snap its 100px-step value to the
nearest tier (900 → `lg`, 1100 → `xl`).

Content sits on a 12-column grid with a `2rem` column gap, `1rem` side padding,
and a `200rem` (2000px) maximum container width.

### Spacing

Spacing runs on two rhythms:

- **Component rhythm — 4px grid** (`spacing.xs`–`spacing.3xl`, 4–40px):
  padding and gaps inside controls, cards, and clusters. `spacing.lg` (20px)
  is the pivot of the whole system — the most-used value on the site and
  identical to the grid column gap.
- **Section rhythm — 10px grid** (`spacing.section-*`, 60–150px): vertical
  spacing between page sections.
- **Optical adjustments** (1–7px) appear freely outside the scale for border
  compensation and icon alignment; leave them untokenized.

## Elevation & Depth

Depth stays light and functional. Floating controls use faint alpha-black
borders, backdrop blur, and soft shadows to separate them from content without
feeling heavy. Raised surfaces should feel frosted and layered, not like hard
cards casting heavy shadows.

- Floating navigation: `12px` backdrop blur with a subtle two-layer shadow.
- Watch button: `24px` backdrop blur with a deeper dark shadow.
- Larger overlay surfaces may use `16px`, `32px`, or `50px` blur.

Named shadows are tokenized in `shadows`:

- `card-soft` and `card-raise` are low-opacity ambient shadows for resting
  cards.
- `card-raise-hover` deepens on hover and is subtly blue-tinted
  (`rgba(10,18,36,…)`) rather than pure black.
- `tooltip` pairs a hairline with a soft drop shadow.
- `glass-highlight` adds an inset top highlight for frosted, glassy controls.
- `hard-offset` (`-3px 3px 0 #000`, with a larger `-6px 6px` variant) is a
  deliberate graphic accent: reserve it for hover moments on light surfaces,
  never as a resting default.

## Motion

Motion is fluid but decisive. The signature easing `motion.ease-site`
(`cubic-bezier(0.87, 0, 0.13, 1)`) drives card and button hovers at roughly
`200ms`.

- Inline links animate only `text-decoration-color` (~250ms,
  `ease-underline`) — no color jump, in line with the restrained-link rule.
- Fades and opacity hovers use `ease-fade` at 150–200ms.
- Larger reveals may extend to `400ms`; nothing in the core UI should feel
  slower than that.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`: stop marquees and sweep animations
  entirely.

## Shapes

Rounded corners are soft but controlled:

- Small controls: `0.8rem`
- CTA buttons: `1.4rem`
- Floating navigation: `1.6rem`
- Privacy link: `2rem`
- Feature media surfaces and cards: `2.4rem`
- Large rounded CTA: `4rem`
- Pill button: `9999px`

The source site names radii by pixel value across a 4–40px range; the tokens
above are the curated subset this project uses.

## Components

- `page` uses the off-white surface and alpha-black body text.
- `hero` uses a near-black surface with pure white title text; the subtitle
  drops to 85% white (`rgba(255,255,255,0.85)`) so the title keeps the top of
  the hierarchy.
- `floating-nav` uses the off-white surface, compact padding, soft corners, and
  navigation label typography.
- `hero-download-button` uses pure white on black text.
- `final-download-button` uses black on pure white text.
- `watch-button` uses a translucent dark surface, pill shape, and compact label
  typography.
- `card` uses a `true-white` surface, media-radius corners, and the
  `card-raise` shadow, deepening to `card-raise-hover` on hover.
- `privacy-link`, `feature-copy`, and `footer` preserve the supporting text
  hierarchy.
- Product imagery should show real interface states or screenshots. Avoid using
  generic decorative imagery where the product itself can carry the section.
- Inline text links should stay visually restrained: use underline emphasis
  rather than a strong color shift.

## Do's and Don'ts

- Do use `#F8F8F8` as Dia's off-white page and navigation surface.
- Do use `true-white` (`#FFFFFF`) only for pure-white surfaces, such as the
  hero CTA and raised cards.
- Do use semantic alpha-black tokens for text, borders, fills, and dividers.
- Do keep component spacing compact and deliberate.
- Do space component internals on the 4px rhythm and page sections on the
  10px rhythm (`spacing.section-*`).
- Do concentrate saturated color in small accent moments.
- Do spend the novelty budget on AI/brand moments; keep core UI patterns
  familiar.
- Do reveal secondary controls on hover or intent instead of showing them
  always.
- Do use the semantic type scale (`h1`–`h4`, `p1`/`p2`, `caption`); display
  styles carry their font-substitution mapping.
- Do keep large headings light (`300`) with tight tracking; don't bold them.
- Do apply the dark block wholesale; don't mix single dark values into light
  mode.
- Do reserve hard-offset shadows for hover moments.
- Do let product screenshots and UI details provide visual proof.
- Don't mix pure white and off-white casually; each has a distinct surface role.
- Don't use saturated accents as large button or section fills.
- Don't create two visual treatments for the same action.
- Don't use copied utility scales as design tokens.
